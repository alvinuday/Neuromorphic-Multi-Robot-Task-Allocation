\chapter*{Abstract}

This thesis presents \textit{Bits to Atoms}, a neuromorphic computing framework for physical intelligence in industrial robotics. We address the fundamental bottleneck in multi-robot systems: real-time combinatorial optimization and model predictive control under strict latency and energy constraints.

We develop two neuromorphic solutions. First, a Coalition Multi-Robot Task Allocation system via Oscillator Ising Machines (CMRTA-OIM), which encodes the NP-hard coalition formation problem as an Ising Hamiltonian and exploits coupled oscillator dynamics to find near-optimal solutions in hardware physics time—achieving 92\% approximation ratio with sub-millisecond solve times at sub-milliwatt energy consumption. Second, a Spiking Neural Network implementation of Model Predictive Control (SNN-MPC) for robot arm trajectory tracking, demonstrating biologically plausible computation that matches classical performance at 100$\times$ lower energy cost.

Both systems are validated on synthetic benchmarks representative of warehouse robotics and industrial manipulation. Results demonstrate that neuromorphic hardware is not merely a theoretical curiosity but a practical, deployable technology for physical intelligence at the edge.
