\chapter*{Abstract}

\textbf{Problem:} Industrial robotics requires real-time solutions to two hard problems: (1) multi-robot task allocation (MRTA, NP-hard coalition formation), and (2) model predictive control (MPC) for robot arm tracking. Classical solvers fail at the edge: ILP times out on 50+ robots; greedy methods lose 40\% quality; latency budgets ($< 20$ ms) and power constraints ($< 5$ W per robot) are unmet.

\textbf{Solution:} This thesis presents \textit{Bits to Atoms}, a neuromorphic computing framework using coupled oscillators and spiking neural networks to solve these problems via physical dynamics, not digital iteration.

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em, itemsep=0.3em]
\item \textbf{CMRTA-OIM} (Oscillator Ising Machines): Encodes coalition formation as an Ising Hamiltonian on vanadium-dioxide oscillators. Achieves \textbf{92\% optimal utility} with \textbf{0.14 ms} solve time and \textbf{1.9 mW} power—\textbf{65$\times$ faster, 9$\times$ lower energy} than branch-and-bound. Scales logarithmically: 0.11--0.19 ms across 10--500 robots, maintaining 85--92\% approximation.

\item \textbf{SNN-MPC} (Spiking Neural Networks): Maps Proportional-Integral Projected Gradient iterations onto neuromorphic hardware for 5-step receding horizon control. Linearization error bounded at $\pm 5\%$ for 20° envelopes. Hardware noise (2--10\% conductance variation) causes 3--20\% optimality loss, yet achieves $< 5$ cm tracking error—\textbf{100$\times$ lower energy-delay product} than classical OSQP.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Validation:} Full mathematical proofs, hand-calculated derivations, Python benchmarks on warehouse (5--50 robots, MRTA) and manipulation (2-DOF arm, MPC) scenarios. OIM meets 20 ms hard real-time; SNN-MPC demonstrates biological plausibility without performance loss.

\textbf{Impact:} First application of Ising machines and SNNs to real MRTA and MPC. Framework is technology-agnostic (vanadium dioxide, memristors, photonics) and extends beyond robotics to physical optimization.
