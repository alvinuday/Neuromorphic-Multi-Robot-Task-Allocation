\chapter{Preface}
\label{ch:preface}

Over the last few years, there has been a drastic shift in what is considered possible in computing. From the Von Neumann bottleneck to GPUs to neuromorphic hardware, the frontier has always been where physics meets computation.

This thesis explores two neuromorphic hardware solutions for robotics: \textbf{Coalition Multi-Robot Task Allocation via Oscillator Ising Machine (OIM)} and \textbf{Model Predictive Control via Spiking Neural Networks (SNN)}. 

The fundamental argument is simple: algorithms are brilliant, hardware is broken. When a robot has 10 milliseconds to decide which coalition handles a task, or 20 milliseconds to compute its next move, classical computing hits a wall. Neuromorphic hardware doesn't hit that wall.

This work validates two concrete approaches using validated mathematics, reproducible experiments, and a vision for Indian manufacturing leadership in the neuromorphic era.

\textit{---Alvin Adarsh Kumar, May 2026}
