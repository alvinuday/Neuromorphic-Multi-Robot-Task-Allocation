\chapter{India's Opportunity in Neuromorphic Manufacturing}
\label{ch:india}

\section{Historical Parallel: Mobile Revolution}

India skipped landline infrastructure and built a mobile-first economy. From near-zero to 800M subscribers in 15 years. Key factors:
\begin{itemize}
\item Low barrier to entry (no copper line infrastructure)
\item Software expertise (Bangalore, Hyderabad)
\item Cost advantage (2$\times$ cheaper than Japan/US)
\item Market size (1.3B population)
\end{itemize}

\section{Neuromorphic Manufacturing Window}

Advanced VLSI requires \$10B+ fabs (TSMC-scale). Neuromorphic devices are different:
\begin{itemize}
\item Analog process: lower precision, smaller penalty for variation
\item Smaller fabs: \$500M--1B feasible
\item VO\textsubscript{2}, FeFET, memristor technologies don't require 3 nm nodes
\item India's semiconductor ecosystem ready
\end{itemize}

Semicon India programme: Rs 76,000 crore (\$9B) investment signal (2021).

\section{India's Assets}

\begin{itemize}
\item Talent: 1.5M engineering graduates annually
\item Physics research: IITB, IISER, national labs
\item Software: world's largest IT services industry
\item Cost structure: 50\% lower than Japan/US
\item Policy: explicit neuromorphic ambition
\end{itemize}
