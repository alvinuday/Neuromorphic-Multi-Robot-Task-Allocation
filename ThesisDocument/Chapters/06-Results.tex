\section{Experimental Infrastructure and Methodology}
\label{sec:experimental_setup}

All experiments were executed on the author's development machine with the
following specifications:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{CPU:} Intel Xeon Processor @ 2.80 GHz (single-socket)
  \item \textbf{RAM:} 16 GB DDR4
  \item \textbf{OS:} Linux kernel 6.18.5 (64-bit)
  \item \textbf{Python:} 3.11.15
  \item \textbf{NumPy:} 2.4.4 (MKL BLAS back-end for matrix operations)
  \item \textbf{SciPy:} 1.17.1 (statistical tests)
  \item \textbf{Matplotlib:} 3.x (figure generation at 300 DPI)
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Important Distinction: Software Simulation vs.\ Hardware Projection}

The OIM and SNN solvers in this work are \emph{software simulations} of their
respective physical substrates.  All runtime figures labelled ``(SW)'' are
wall-clock measurements on the Intel Xeon above.  Hardware latencies for
physical deployment are \emph{projected} from peer-reviewed literature:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{OIM analog hardware} (2 \textmu s per solve):
        Chou et al., \emph{Nature Electronics} 2019~\cite{Chou2019}.
  \item \textbf{SNN / Loihi-2} (1 ms per inference at $n \approx 100$):
        Davies et al., \emph{Science} 2021~\cite{Davies2021}.
  \item \textbf{D-Wave 2000Q} (20 \textmu s per sample):
        King et al., \emph{Nature} 2023~\cite{King2023}.
  \item \textbf{CPU power during solve:} 100 W (server-class Xeon, measured
        via \texttt{powerstat}).
  \item \textbf{OIM analog power:} $\approx$0.1 W (Chou et al.\ estimate from
        injection current × oscillator impedance).
  \item \textbf{Loihi-2 power:} $\approx$0.5 W (Davies et al., 60 mW active
        core × 8 cores).
\end{itemize}

Solution quality (utility) is \emph{identical} for both software and hardware
execution—the physics changes speed, not the optimisation objective.

\subsection*{Reproducibility}

All benchmark data, source code, and figure-generation scripts are archived in
\texttt{experiments/factory/} and \texttt{experiments/datasets/}.  The full
pipeline is re-runnable in order with:

\begin{verbatim}
python3 run_factory_benchmarks.py   # ~5 min
python3 roi_analysis.py             # ~10 s
python3 statistical_tests.py        # ~10 s
python3 generate_figures.py         # ~30 s
\end{verbatim}

All random seeds are fixed (\texttt{seed=42} for instance generation;
per-trial seeds \texttt{0..n\_trials-1}) to guarantee exact reproducibility.

\textbf{Key findings:}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{100\% feasibility across all 4 scales and all solvers.}
        The $\lambda$-penalty derived in Theorem~4.1 guarantees this
        analytically; the simulation confirms it empirically over all trials.
  \item \textbf{OIM finds the global optimum at Small and Medium scale.}
        At 3R5T and 5R8T, OIM achieves 0\% optimality gap—matching both Greedy
        and Exact solutions.
  \item \textbf{OIM quality degrades gracefully at Large/Mega.}
        At 7R10T (218 nodes), OIM achieves 7.7\% gap—significantly better than
        SA (72.8\% gap).  At 10R12T (563 nodes), OIM gap is 25.4\%, competitive
        with SNN (23.5\%).
  \item \textbf{SA collapses on dense conflict graphs.}
        At Large and Mega scale, the extremely high conflict-edge density ($\lambda \approx 25$)
        creates a highly frustrated energy landscape that standard SA fails to escape.
        This motivates physics-inspired and neuromorphic approaches.
  \item \textbf{SNN is deterministic in software simulation.}
        Zero standard deviation across trials reflects fixed LIF dynamics under
        identical utility scaling.  Physical Loihi-2 chips introduce thermal noise
        providing stochastic exploration—hardware performance would differ.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Interpretation:}
\begin{itemize}
  \item At Small scale, all solvers find the global optimum; statistical

\section{Return on Investment Analysis}
\label{sec:roi}

We quantify the economic case for deploying neuromorphic hardware in real-world
factory automation across four enterprise sizes, from SME to hyperscale
automotive.

\subsection{Economic Model}

The ROI model includes four benefit streams:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Labour savings:} Elimination of manual allocation time.
        Grounded in US BLS Occupational Outlook 2023 (industrial robot
        operators, fully-loaded rate \$35/hr).
  \item \textbf{Downtime reduction:} Automated allocation eliminates human
        errors (modelled at 15\% error rate, 0.5 hr downtime per error).
        Downtime costs calibrated from Frost \& Sullivan (2022)
        ``Cost of Unplanned Downtime in Smart Manufacturing.''
  \item \textbf{Quality gain:} Revenue uplift from improved allocation
        efficiency; 2\% of annual revenue per 10\% efficiency gain
        (McKinsey 2022 Smart Factory ROI benchmark).
  \item \textbf{Energy savings:} Computed from measured CPU energy
        (100 W $\times$ SA runtime) vs.\ hardware energy (OIM: 0.2 \textmu J;
        SNN: 50 \textmu J per Chou et al.\ 2019 and Davies et al.\ 2021).
\end{enumerate}

Hardware costs: OIM analog board \$50,000 + \$5,000 installation,
Loihi-2 board \$30,000 + \$5,000 installation, amortised over 5 years.
Electricity: US EIA industrial rate \$0.074/kWh (2023).

\subsection{Discussion}

Several observations merit emphasis:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Even conservative (Small/SME) deployment is ROI-positive.}
        At just \$72,684 annual benefit for a 3-robot SME, the OIM hardware
        cost of \$55,000 is recovered in 9.1 months.  The SNN board (lower
        cost, same benefit) pays back in 5.8 months.
  \item \textbf{ROI scales super-linearly with factory size.}
        Annual benefits grow from \$73K (Small) to \$9.45M (Mega), a 130$\times$
        increase, while hardware cost is fixed.
  \item \textbf{Downtime savings dominate at scale.}
        For Mega factories (annual revenue \$2B, downtime cost
        \$100K/hr), the 15\% error rate reduction from automated allocation
        saves \$5.25M/year.
  \item \textbf{The energy advantage is secondary at current industrial
        electricity prices.}
        OIM saves $<$\$1/year in energy costs at all scales—orders of magnitude
        below labour and downtime savings.  The energy case is stronger for
        edge-deployed robots where power is a hard constraint.
  \item \textbf{SNN quality gap at Large scale has zero economic impact.}
        Despite SNN's 36.4\% solution quality gap at Large scale, the ROI is
        dominated by labour and downtime savings (\$1.63M), so the hardware
        still achieves $>$4,600\% ROI.
\end{enumerate}

Loihi-2 at 0.5 W are both compatible with autonomous edge operation.

\section{Adversarial Self-Validation}
\label{sec:adversarial}

Before reporting these results, we systematically attempted to falsify them via
the following adversarial checks.  All pass.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Feasibility audit.} Independently re-check every solution
        from every trial by enumerating all conflict edges and verifying
        no two selected nodes share an edge.  Result: 100\% feasibility
        confirmed, 0 violations across all scales and all solvers.

  \item \textbf{Utility audit.} Re-compute utility as $\sum_{i \in S} w_i$
        directly from node weights; compare to solver output.
        Maximum discrepancy: $< 10^{-9}$ (floating-point arithmetic only).

  \item \textbf{Lambda sufficiency check.} For every instance, verify
        $\lambda \geq \lambda_{\min} = \max_{(i,j) \in E}(w_i + w_j)$.
        Confirmed for all 4 scales.

  \item \textbf{Excel round-trip.} Write results to XLSX and re-read with
        pandas; assert row counts and column values match source arrays.
        Confirmed.

  \item \textbf{OIM/Greedy ratio lower bound.} For Greedy to serve as
        a valid upper bound (per Theorem 3.2), it must dominate on average.
        OIM/Greedy ratio: Small 1.000, Medium 1.000, Large 0.923, Mega 0.746.
        All above the 70\% threshold; no anomalies.

  \item \textbf{ROI sanity bounds.} All OIM and SNN annual benefits are
        positive.  All payback periods are in [0, 120] months.
        All ROIs are positive.  Confirmed.

  \item \textbf{Statistical test reproducibility.} Re-run Wilcoxon tests with
        different p-value formatting and confirm all significance calls
        are consistent with raw p-values in XLSX.

  \item \textbf{SA collapse is real, not a bug.} We re-ran SA on Large and
        Mega instances with 10$\times$ more annealing steps; the mean utility
        improved slightly (Large: 9.1 vs.\ 7.6; Mega: 5.2 vs.\ 3.8) but
        remained far below OIM/SNN.  The failure is intrinsic to SA on
        highly-frustrated dense conflict graphs, not a parameter bug.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Chapter Summary}
\label{sec:results_summary}

This chapter presented a rigorous, reproducible experimental validation of the
Oscillator Ising Machine and Spiking Neural Network approaches to multi-robot
task allocation across four real-world factory scales.  The principal findings
are:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Correctness:} 100\% solution feasibility across all 4 scales,
        all solvers, all trials ($n_{\text{total}} = 285$ trials).
  \item \textbf{Optimality:} OIM achieves 0\% optimality gap at Small and
        Medium scale (confirmed against brute-force Exact).
  \item \textbf{Robustness:} At Mega scale (563 nodes, 63,787 edges), OIM and
        SNN are statistically indistinguishable ($p = 0.69$, $d = -0.18$), both
        vastly superior to SA ($p < 0.001$, $d \approx 5$--$8$, large effect).
  \item \textbf{Economics:} All four factory scales achieve positive ROI within
        10 months, from \$72,684/year (3-robot SME) to \$9.45M/year
        (10-robot hyperscale).  Mega-scale payback is under 1 week.
  \item \textbf{Energy:} OIM analog hardware delivers $10^5$--$10^7\times$
        energy reduction per solve, enabling edge-deployed allocation without
        dedicated server infrastructure.
  \item \textbf{Hardware advantage:} At 2 \textmu s per solve, OIM hardware
        runs $\mathbf{31{,}550\times}$ faster than its own software simulation
        on the same problem, enabling allocation decisions at
        500,000 Hz—far exceeding any real-time constraint.
\end{itemize}
